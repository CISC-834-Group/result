# Firefox experiment results

We compare the supplied Mozilla baseline with two Firefox cohorts: a human proxy from November 30, 2021 through November 29, 2022, and AI candidates from November 1, 2025 through October 10, 2026. For each new cohort, we build a general model and models for its five largest products. We also build AI models for Toolkit and DevTools to support comparisons with the human products. Including the baseline, we evaluate 15 models.

The table below presents the general-model results. RQ1 and RQ2 metrics are averaged across ten cross-validation folds. Bug-inducing changes account for 11.2% of the human cohort and 8.8% of the AI-candidate cohort, according to the SZZ labels.

| Dataset | Changes | Bug-inducing changes | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | ROC-AUC | EALR recall at 20% effort |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original Mozilla | 98,275 | 5,149 | 76.4% | 13.4% | 63.9% | 0.774 | 14.4% |
| Human proxy (2021–2022) | 28,459 | 3,184 | 62.5% | 16.6% | 58.2% | 0.646 | 80.8% |
| AI candidates (2025–2026) | 9,603 | 845 | 66.4% | 15.3% | 62.0% | 0.695 | 65.3% |

## RQ1: How well can we predict bug-inducing changes?

**Approach.** To answer RQ1, we use the supplied logistic regression procedure to predict whether a change introduces a defect. We evaluate the models using ten-fold cross-validation and report accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and ROC-AUC. To address class imbalance, we apply majority-class undersampling to the training folds. We do not resample the test folds.

**Results.** We find that the general model for AI candidates achieves an AUC of 0.695, compared with 0.646 for the human model and 0.774 for the Mozilla baseline. The human model has the highest precision (16.6%), while the Mozilla baseline has the highest recall (63.9%). Precision ranges from 13.4% to 16.6% across the three models, indicating that many flagged changes do not have a positive defect label.

These results describe how well the models predict defects in the selected datasets. They do not show that AI use improves code quality. We have not tested the statistical significance of these differences. Results for all product models are reported in `results_summary.csv`.

## RQ2: Does prioritizing changes reduce inspection effort?

**Approach.** To account for the effort required to inspect changes, we evaluate the supplied LR and effort-aware linear regression (EALR) models using ten-fold cross-validation. We measure the proportion of labeled bug-inducing changes identified using 20% of the total estimated inspection effort. We also report Popt. We calculate effort and evaluation metrics using the replication package.

**Results.** At 20% estimated effort, EALR identifies 80.8% of labeled bug-inducing changes in the human cohort and 65.3% in the AI-candidate cohort. The corresponding value for the supplied Mozilla baseline is 14.4%. Thus, EALR achieves the highest recall for the human cohort when inspection effort is limited to 20%.

These results differ from RQ1 because effort-aware evaluation considers the cost of inspecting changes as well as their predicted risk. However, the cohorts also differ in period, product mix and change characteristics, which limits direct comparison. The LR and EALR results, including Popt, are reported in `results_summary.csv`.

## RQ3: Which factors are associated with bug-inducing changes?

**Approach.** To examine the factors associated with defects, we fit the supplied regression models on each full dataset, separately from the cross-validation used in RQ1 and RQ2. We examine the signs of the selected logistic regression coefficients and report their odds ratios. We also report the EALR coefficients.

**Results.** NF and FIX have positive coefficients in all three general logistic models, while AGE has a negative coefficient. EXP has a negative coefficient in the Mozilla and AI-candidate models but is not selected in the human model. NF, FIX and AGE therefore have the same direction of association across the general models, while EXP is selected in only two models.

Each coefficient must be interpreted in relation to the other factors included in the model. Coefficient magnitudes depend on the scales of the factors and cannot be used directly to rank their importance. The associations do not establish causation. Full logistic coefficients, odds ratios and EALR coefficients are reported in `rq3_coefficients.csv`.

## Threats to validity

**Cohort definitions.** The AI screening rules use broad keyword matching and author propagation. Under author propagation, we also flag subsequent commits by the author of a flagged commit. The same rules flag 12.7% of a 2019–2020 comparison cohort. Only 16 direct matches in the current AI window satisfy explicit attribution patterns, such as “Co-authored-by” and “generated by.” These matches have not been manually verified as evidence of AI authorship. We therefore describe this cohort as AI candidates. Similarly, the human window predates ChatGPT's public release but does not provide a verified authorship label.

**Defect labels and comparability.** Missing issue links or reports affect 177 human changes (0.6%) and 333 AI changes (3.5%). The labels also depend on the assumptions of SZZ. Since we do not observe fixes after each window ends, some defect-inducing changes may remain unlabeled. Differences in period and product composition may contribute to the observed model differences. These comparisons cannot isolate the effect of AI use.

**Sparse product models.** Nine model stages produce separation or nonconvergence warnings, which are retained in the results table. The AI Toolkit and DevTools subsets contain only 16 and 18 bug-inducing changes, respectively. Their results should be treated as exploratory. `METHOD.md` describes the model procedures and the F1 calculation correction.
